# Steph Curry — Training & Fitness Directory

## Training Philosophy

- "It helps you become a smarter basketball player" — on neurocognitive training that combines physical drills with sensory overload
- "Whatever felt right, he kept in the routine, other things, he discarded" — his pre-game routine developed organically over years, not through rigid planning
- His trainer Keke Lyles: "Shiftiness is an ankle strategy, but power comes from the hips." — the single insight that saved Curry's career
- Keke Lyles: "He's probably ten times stronger than what people think... Curry can deadlift 400 pounds... Steph's the second strongest on our team pulling the deadlift."
- Brandon Payne's core principle: simulate game-like situations in training — every drill mimics the speed, pressure, and decision-making of actual games
- Strength coach Carl Bergstrom: "The key is vertical core strength" — the foundation for Curry's ball handling and shooting

- **GPS accelerometer tracking**: Warriors use wearable tech to monitor load and fatigue, managing Curry's minutes (averaged 33.8 min/game during peak years)

## Pre-Game Routine

Curry's pre-game ritual takes ~20 minutes and begins 90 minutes before tipoff. It developed organically — "whatever felt right, he kept; other things, he discarded."

### Sequence:

1. **Game-day breakfast**: Consistent eggs/omelet with spinach and breakfast meats, yogurt with granola and berries, protein smoothie
2. **Pre-game nap**: Sleeps before heading to arena
3. **Pre-game playlist**: Listens to music to set mental tone
4. **Ball-handling drills** (15-20 minutes): Figure 8s, behind-the-back crosses, one-hand behind-the-back movements. Evolved from stationary to on-the-move series of crossovers. All done at game speed.
5. **Shooting warm-up** (100+ shots): Progressive method — starts with form shooting near the basket, gradually moves outward. Practices spot-up threes, dribble pull-ups, and floaters. Has been filmed making 16 consecutive shots without hitting the rim.
6. **Tunnel shot**: Since 2013, shoots from the tunnel while heading back to the locker room. Warriors staff member Curtis passes him the ball. Adapted the shot when Chase Center's tunnel was blocked.
7. **100 Meter Dash**: Sprints from tunnel to locker room.
8. **"Lock In" tweet**: Posts to social media as final mental switch into game mode.
9. **Pre-game meal**: PB&J sandwich close to tipoff.

## Diet & Nutrition

### Game-Day Meals

**Breakfast** (consistent every game day):
- Egg omelet or scramble with breakfast meats and spinach
- Bowl of yogurt with granola and mixed berries
- Protein smoothie
- Sometimes half a waffle for extra carbs

**Quote**: "I eat pretty much the same breakfast every time. It's some type of egg, an omelet, or like a scramble, something with some breakfast meats in there and some spinach."

**Pre-game meal**: Protein (fish or chicken) + pasta or rice + vegetables. Ayesha's pasta sauce with San Marzano tomatoes, garlic, basil, bay leaves, and eggplant is a staple.

**Pre-game snack**: PB&J sandwich

### Regular Diet

- **Gluten-free**: Curry has publicly referenced eating gluten-free as part of "eating healthier"
- **Whole foods emphasis**: Fresh fruits (strawberries, raspberries, apples), green vegetables (kale, broccoli, zucchini, bell pepper, spinach)
- **Lunch prep**: ~10 meal containers prepared around 10 AM with vegetables, protein, and sweet potatoes
- **Snacks**: Nuts and fruits throughout the day
- **Dinner**: Family meal around 6:30-7:00 PM — favorites include Ayesha's chicken parmigiana and Spanish-rub chicken with parsley-mint sauce
- **Philosophy**: Curry describes following an "eat whatever you can" approach to maintain necessary caloric intake, but increasingly prioritizes nutrition for recovery
- **Ayesha's influence**: Wife is a NYT bestselling cookbook author and James Beard Award-nominated chef — meals emphasize quinoa, grilled chicken, pasta with fresh vegetables

### Supplements
- Whey protein (muscle maintenance)
- Creatine (stamina and energy)
- Omega-3s (brain health)

## Sleep & Recovery

### Sleep

- **Schedule**: 11:00 PM to 8:00 AM (9 hours in bed, targeting 8 hours of sleep)
- **No screens after 10:30 PM**: Adopted on advice from sleep specialist Cheri Mah
- **Philosophy**: Curry prioritizes sleep over nightlife — believes consistent sleep gives him a competitive edge

### Recovery Methods

**Sensory Deprivation Float Tanks**:
- Uses Reboot Float Spa in San Francisco
- Floats in body-temperature water (93.5°F) saturated with Epsom salts and magnesium
- Sessions last ~1 hour
- Aims for once every two weeks (schedule permitting)
- Benefits: muscle relaxation from minerals, mental decluttering, focus enhancement
- Curry has become the poster child for float therapy in professional sports

**Other Recovery**:
- Foam rolling before every training session (non-negotiable)
- Stretching and flexibility work (pre- and post-workout)
- Soft tissue restoration therapy
- Yoga for flexibility and mindfulness
- Epsom salt baths
- Visualization and mental rehearsal of successful shots and plays
- Afternoon nap (~5:00 PM on off days)

## Ankle Rebuild

### The Problem
- 17 total ankle injuries throughout career
- 2011-12 season: 5 sprains in 26 games — career appeared over
- First surgery: pre-2011-12 season in Charlotte, NC (ligament reconstruction)
- Second surgery: April 25, 2012 at Southern California Orthopedic Institute (Dr. Richard Ferkel) — arthroscopic debridement removing scar tissue, bone spurs, and cartilage chips

### The Solution

**Architect**: Keke Lyles, Warriors Director of Athletic Performance (joined 2013)

**Core insight**: "Shiftiness is an ankle strategy, but power comes from the hips." Lyles theorized that building lower-body hip/glute strength would take significant stress off the ankles.

**The Program**:
1. Centered training around the trap bar deadlift — progressed from 200 lbs to 400 lbs over ~2 years
2. Single-leg strength exercises became the foundation: single-leg deadlifts, Bulgarian split squats, single-leg reverse lunges
3. Hip hinge movement patterns to teach glute/hamstring activation
4. Single-leg hip airplane (yoga pose) for balance and hip strength
5. Core strengthening to support the entire kinetic chain
6. Balance work on Airex pad with vision obstruction and strobe goggles

**Supporting Equipment**:
- Zamst ankle braces worn in every game
- Custom Under Armour sneakers scientifically designed for his feet
- GPS accelerometers tracking fatigue and load

### The Result
- From 5 sprains in 26 games (2011-12) to only 2 games missed for ankle issues over the next 2.5 years
- Won back-to-back MVP awards (2015, 2016 — first unanimous MVP in history)
- Maintained weight at ~190 lbs (avoided adding bulk that would stress ankles)
- Transformed from injury-plagued player to 2× MVP, Finals MVP (2024), 8× All-Star, 4× NBA Champion

## Neurocognitive Training (Brandon Payne)

This is the "secret weapon" that separates Curry's training from every other NBA player:

**FITLIGHT System**: Colored lights mounted on walls that flash during drills. Curry must read the light colors while executing dribble moves — associating specific lights with specific moves.

**Strobe Goggles (Eclipse by Sensory Performance Technology)**: Force sensory overload during regular drills. The goggles intermittently block vision, requiring Curry to process visual information in fragments — building faster processing speed.

**Combined Drill Example**: In a seven-spot shooting drill, a shot only counts as a "make" if Curry makes the basket AND correctly reads the lights. This overloads the brain with simultaneous physical and cognitive demands.

**Impact**: After adopting stroboscopic training post-2014-15 season, Curry's made three-pointers increased by 116 in the following season. Payne credits the goggles as "one of the biggest reasons" Curry broke the three-point record and became the first unanimous MVP.

**Philosophy**: "Overload the sensory system and nervous system" so that actual games feel slower and simpler by comparison.
