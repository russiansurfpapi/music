# Listening Guide

## Why the 3% chains are short (on purpose)

Each chain is **5–6 tracks / ~25 minutes**. That's a *teaching module*, not a playlist you loop. You listen through once, in order, and the lesson is the sequence itself — you hear how one sound becomes another. A longer chain dilutes the causation. The whole point is that you can feel the 3% shift at each step.

Think of them like lectures. Your job isn't to press shuffle; it's to play it start-to-finish and notice what stays the same vs what moves.

Your 113 `🎧 Study ·` playlists are the opposite format — those are immersion baths, 20–500 tracks, put them on and stay put. Two different tools.

---

## What to listen for (in general)

Every time the chain steps forward in time, ask:
1. **What's constant?** (the groove, the break, the arp, the riddim)
2. **What shifted 3%?** (tempo, EQ, who's on top, production tech, feeling)
3. **How would you describe the new thing *without* using genre labels?**

That third question is the ear-training. You're moving from "this is tech house" to "this is four-on-the-floor at 124 with a dry kick and filtered vocal chops" — the DJ's actual language.

---

## Recommended order

Order is built for where **you** are (41% house, 18% hip-hop, 9% disco/soul, 86% post-2010). Start with chains that teach the *roots* of genres you already live in. Graduate to chains that open doors you haven't walked through.

### Phase 1 — Know thyself (genres you already love, roots you haven't heard)

These teach you the backbone of music you already like. You'll recognize the modern end and the ancestor will feel revelatory.

1. **🎧 3% · THE FUNKY DRUMMER NEVER STOPPED**
   - *Why first:* you love hip-hop; you've probably never sat with the 1970 James Brown break that built it.
   - *Listen for:* the snare ghost-note on "and of 4." Every sample keeps it.
   - *Vocabulary you should leave with:* "break," "ghost note," "sampled vs interpolated."

2. **🎧 3% · CHICAGO IS A PRAYER**
   - *Why second:* you're 41% house. This is house's original theology.
   - *Listen for:* the church-chord progression; how the machine kick replaces the drummer but keeps the *feeling* of gospel.
   - *Vocabulary:* "909," "deep house lineage," "the drum machine as spiritual instrument."

3. **🎧 3% · I FEEL LOVE IS STILL HAPPENING**
   - *Why third:* this is the arp (16th-note synth pattern) that underwrites disco → hi-NRG → house → synth pop → hyperpop. You've heard its echo in Charli XCX and Beyoncé without knowing.
   - *Listen for:* the arpeggio is *exactly the same idea* 47 years apart.
   - *Vocabulary:* "Moog arpeggio," "propulsion," "Moroder as patient zero."

### Phase 2 — Trace one thread through many rooms

Now you start crossing rooms. Same element, different genre.

4. **🎧 3% · DEMBOW IS A NATION**
   - Jamaica → Panama → Puerto Rico → Colombia → modern house-inflected Latin.
   - *Listen for:* the BOOM-ch-boom-CHICK pattern survives even when the style changes completely.
   - *Vocabulary:* "dembow riddim," "dancehall → reggaeton," "why Bad Bunny sounds like Shabba Ranks."

5. **🎧 3% · THE MACHINE THAT REFUSED**
   - A failed bassline synth (TB-303) invented acid house, acid techno, French house, and accidentally keeps returning.
   - *Listen for:* the resonance knob. That squelch *is* the instrument.
   - *Vocabulary:* "303," "acid," "filter resonance," "the 1987 Chicago accident."

6. **🎧 3% · AMEN, BROTHER**
   - Six seconds of 1969 built jungle, drum & bass, breakcore. The single most-remixed drum break in history.
   - *Listen for:* the snare hits on 2 AND 3 (double-hit). When producers chop the break, they're rearranging this pattern.
   - *Vocabulary:* "amen break," "time-stretched," "breakcore," "jungle's debt to Clyde Stubblefield."

### Phase 3 — Build lateral knowledge

The chains you'd appreciate most once Phase 1+2 ears are tuned.

7. **🎧 3% · TRANS-EUROPE TO DETROIT**
   - Kraftwerk (Düsseldorf, 1977) → Afrofuturist Detroit techno. The Belleville Three treated Kraftwerk as *soul music for robots*. That reframe is the whole story.
   - *Listen for:* European strictness + Black feeling. The melancholy is Detroit's, not Germany's.
   - *Vocabulary:* "Belleville Three," "Afrofuturism," "machine soul."

8. **🎧 3% · THINK (ABOUT IT)**
   - Lyn Collins' 1972 yell built half of late-80s hip-hop. "Yeah! Woo!" is a 2-second vocal stab everyone quoted.
   - *Listen for:* the exclamation shows up as a *rhythmic instrument* — dropped on beats like a drum hit.
   - *Vocabulary:* "vocal stab," "Brown/Collins lineage," "the break as building block."

9. **🎧 3% · SOUTH LONDON AT 140**
   - Original dubstep. Before the drop was a product, it was a room with subwoofers.
   - *Listen for:* *space*. The silence between the kick and the bass weight is the whole genre. Later dubstep filled that silence with noise; these six tracks keep it.
   - *Vocabulary:* "half-time feel," "sub-bass weight," "FWD>>," "the original vs. the brostep bastardization."

10. **🎧 3% · FRENCH TOUCH IS A FILTER**
    - Cosmic disco → Daft Punk → Todd Terje. One Korg filter sweep across 45 years.
    - *Listen for:* the "band-pass" sound — disco being *compressed* into something tighter. The sweep is the lesson.
    - *Vocabulary:* "French touch," "filter sweep," "disco loop as sample source."

## What to do once you've done all 10

You'll have vocabulary for roughly: **break, arp, sweep, riddim, 303, 808/909, ghost note, half-time, four-on-the-floor, sub-bass, sample vs interpolation, deep, raw, warm, cold, propulsive, filtered, machine soul.**

That's the spine. Now graduate to:

1. **The Focus playlists** — pick a subgenre the 3% chains pointed you toward (e.g. Detroit techno after *Trans-Europe to Detroit*) and immerse. These run 50–500 tracks; play them over days, not hours.

2. **The A/B playlists** — once you have vocabulary, the distinctions sharpen. *Deep house vs tech house* won't feel arbitrary anymore.

3. **Evolution playlists** — chronological 50-80 tracks per genre. These are what the chains hinted at, expanded.

4. **Reverse-engineer on your own** — when a new track hits you, ask: *what's the break? what's the riddim? what's the 3% shift from its ancestor?* If you can answer without looking, the ear training worked.

---

## Listening posture

- **Headphones > speakers** for chains. You need to hear detail.
- **One chain per session.** Don't stack — the crossfade between lessons blurs them.
- **Commute and walks work fine for Focus/Evolution playlists**, but chains need attention. 25 minutes of actually listening.
- **Re-listen to each chain a week later.** First pass you hear the tracks; second pass you hear the lineage.

---

## If a chain doesn't land

Some will hit harder than others. That's fine — it tells you where your ear is. If *Think (About It)* feels like nothing and *South London at 140* rearranges your brain, that's data about where to dig deeper.

Flag the chains that don't land. We can swap tracks or build adjacent chains that connect to them from angles that fit your taste better.
