# DJ Vocabulary Glossary

A curated reference for hearing and talking about dance/electronic music the way working DJs do. Three layers: **subgenre distinctions**, **track-internal structure**, and **set-level arc**. The companion `dj_glossary.yaml` has the structured data; this file is for reading and internalizing.

---

## Part 1 — Subgenre Distinctions That Matter

The point here isn't "house vs techno." It's the fine lines — the ones working DJs hear immediately and festival programmers argue about.

### House family

**Chicago house** (1984-87) is 909/707-driven, raw, percussive, warehouse-rough. It predates acid house. **Acid house** (1986, Phuture) is the same lineage but centered on the TB-303 — the squelching filter-automated bassline is the *whole point*. Tempo 115-125 BPM, still swung and soulful. **Acid techno** (London, mid-90s) takes the 303 and speeds it to 135-150+ BPM with cold, distorted aggression — strips away the house soul entirely.

**Deep house** is the Larry Heard lineage — "Can You Feel It," "Mystery of Love" — jazzy 7th/9th chords, soft Rhodes pads, muted basslines, 110-125 BPM, soulful. Important distinction: the modern Beatport "deep tech" or "deep house" label often means something closer to tech house with a darker filter. True deep house is chordal and emotional; modern "deep" is stripped-down and percussion-first. ([RA Larry Heard history](https://thissongissick.com/post/dance-music-history-101-larry-heard/), [Mixmag](https://mixmag.net/feature/humble-hero-house-pioneer-larry-heard-is-the-house-pioneer-best-left-unsolved))

**Tech house** (UK, late 90s) is house's rhythmic bones with techno's sparseness — tight dry percussion, rolling locked bass, 125-128 BPM. **Microhouse** (Kompakt/Perlon, early 2000s) replaces the house drum palette with clicks, static, vinyl crackle, and tiny vocal chops — still funky underneath the glitches. **Minimal techno** (Robert Hood, Hawtin) is colder than both — mid-frequency focused, linear, 3-5 sounds for an entire track, focused on "rhythm and repetition instead of melody." ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimal_techno), [Microhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microhouse))

**Afro house** (South Africa, 90s) is 120-125 BPM with polyrhythmic log-drum percussion, Zulu/Xhosa vocal influences. **Amapiano** (Gauteng, mid-2010s) is slower — 112-115 BPM — with log-drum-as-lead-bass, jazzy Rhodes chords, a lounging feel. **Gqom** (Durban, 2010s) is the dark cousin: broken rhythm (not 4/4), drone-bass, sparse hammering kicks, almost no melody. ([RA Afro House](https://ra.co/features/4440), [Afroplug](https://afroplug.com/amapiano-vs-afrobeats-understanding-the-difference/), [Gqom on Bandcamp Daily](https://daily.bandcamp.com/scene-report/gqom-club-sound-label-profile))

### Techno family

**Detroit techno** (Atkins/May/Saunderson, mid-80s) is warm and melodic — Motown string sensibility over 808/909 machines. **Berlin techno** (Tresor-era onward) is colder, harder, more functional. **Dub techno** (Basic Channel, 1993) is the reverb-drenched subgenre — chord stabs swimming in long delay, the "thawing ice" aesthetic rooted in Jamaican dub. Chain Reaction carries this forward. ([Basic Channel on RA](https://ra.co/features/3365))

**Peak-time techno** (130-138 BPM) is big-room rolling groove built for the biggest hour of a set. **Hard techno** (140-160+) is faster and abrasive, distorted kicks and screaming leads — Sara Landry, SPFDJ. **Industrial techno** (Perc, Blawan) emphasizes metallic texture and mid-range noise over speed. ([T.A.M.R.](https://www.theacidmind.com/2025/01/the-top-djs-in-peak-time-raw-hypnotic-hard-techno/))

### UK / bass continuum

**UK garage** (mid-90s, London) is 130-135 BPM shuffled R&B-influenced house. **2-step** is the subset where kicks 2 and 4 are removed. **Speed garage** keeps 4/4 and adds jungle-reese basslines. From this soup came **grime** (East London, early 2000s, 140 BPM, MC-forward, detuned-square-wave "Eskibeat" sound — Wiley is the godfather) and **dubstep** (Croydon/FWD>>, 140 BPM halftime, sub-bass *weight*, Mala/Coki/Skream). ([Grime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grime_music), [Eskibeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eskimo_(grime_beat)), [Dubstep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubstep))

Critical distinction the user cares about: **original dubstep** emphasizes *sub-bass you feel in your chest* and dub-influenced space. **Brostep** (Skrillex/Rusko, late 2000s) moves bass to the mid-range — "robotic/metal-esque aggression" designed for festival PAs. Same 140 BPM halftime, completely different sound ideology. ([VIPZONE](https://www.vipzone-samples.com/en/brostep-vs-dubstep/))

**Jungle** (160-170 BPM, chopped Amen breaks, reggae bass) begat **drum & bass** (174 BPM). DnB subgenres matter: **liquid** (smooth/jazzy/vocal), **jump-up** (bouncy cartoon "foghorn" bass, crowd-service), **neurofunk** (glitchy, morphing reese basses, dystopian sci-fi). ([Drum and Bass UK](https://drumandbassuk.com/news/article/drum-and-bass-subgenres-explained))

### US bass / club

**Footwork/juke** (Chicago, RP Boo/DJ Rashad) is 160 BPM with a triplet/syncopated feel, chopped stuttered vocals, sparse abstract patterns built for dance battles. **Baltimore club** (~130 BPM, 8/4 beat, looped vocal snippets — yes including SpongeBob), **Jersey club** (smoother B'more descendant with signature triplet kick pattern / "bed squeak" sample), **ballroom** (samples MAW's "The Ha Dance" for the vogue "dip"). ([Bandcamp Daily footwork](https://daily.bandcamp.com/lists/footwork-chicago-list), [Vice club taxonomy](https://www.vice.com/en/article/you-think-you-know-but-you-have-no-idea-the-difference-between-baltimore-philly-and-jersey-club-2/))

### Electro / IDM / Trance

**Electro** (the real thing — Bambaataa, Cybotron, Drexciya, Dopplereffekt) is defined by the syncopated TR-808 pattern, not 4/4. Don't confuse with **electroclash** (late-90s/early-2000s European revival with punk vocals). **IDM** (Warp's Artificial Intelligence series, Aphex/Autechre) is home-listening electronica with chopped breakbeats. **Goa trance** (early 90s India) is warmer, melodic, long evolving arpeggios; **psytrance** is cleaner, compressed, sharper — built for club PAs. ([XLR8R electro](https://xlr8r.com/features/electro-everlasting/), [Psychedelic Island](https://psychedelicisland.com/articles/goa-trance-or-psytrance/))

### Disco lineage

**Italo disco** (Italy, early 80s) is drum-machine + synth bass, vocoders, accented English. **Cosmic/space disco** (Baldelli, Baia degli Angeli) is DJs pitching disco DOWN to ~100-110 BPM and blending with African/electronic records. **Balearic** is Ibiza-era genre-agnostic selection (DJ Alfredo at Amnesia). **Nu-disco** is the 2000s Scandinavian revival (Lindstrøm, Todd Terje). ([Italo on Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/a-bullshitter39s-guide-to-italo-disco-us-translation/), [Nu-disco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu-disco))

### Latin / Afro / global

**Reggaeton** is built on the dembow riddim (3+3+2 tresillo) at ~90-96 BPM feeling like double-time. **Dominican dembow** is faster (120-130 BPM), rawer, chant-led. **Funk carioca** (Rio) was classically 130 BPM tamborzão; the "150 BPM funk" wave (Rennan da Penha, 2017+) speeds it up to a pounding sub-kick drive. **Afrobeats** (Nigeria/Ghana) is 100-115 BPM pop-song-form with triplet swing; **amapiano** is South African instrumental house-derivative — they're often confused but are structurally very different. ([RBMA dembow](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2013/07/dembow-a-loop-history/), [Berklee dembow](https://www.berklee.edu/berklee-now/news/what-is-dembow-tracing-the-roots-of-a-global-phenomenon), [Bandcamp funk carioca](https://daily.bandcamp.com/lists/the-manic-joyous-sound-of-brazils-funk-carioca))

### Hip-hop (meaningful subset)

**Boom bap** (East Coast, late 80s-mid 90s) = heavy kick + crisp snare at 85-95 BPM, jazz/soul samples, lyrics sit high. **Trap** (Atlanta, early 2000s) = rolling pitched 808 bass, rapid hi-hat triplets, halftime feel (snare on beat 3), 130-170 BPM nominal. **Drill** descended from trap but uses skippy syncopated hi-hats "echoing machine-gun fire" and sliding 808s — Chicago origin, then UK (808Melo) and Brooklyn variants. **Cloud rap** (Clams Casino era) is hazy, reverb-drenched, chopped female-vocal samples, floating 808s. ([NPR on trap](https://www.npr.org/2023/08/10/1193297765/the-birth-of-trap-music-and-the-rise-of-southern-hip-hop), [Grammy subgenres](https://www.grammy.com/news/11-subgenres-of-hip-hop-jersey-club-drill-trap-examples-videos))

## Part 2 — Track-Internal Structure

Every 4/4 dance track is a conversation in 8s and 16s. **Phrases** are the unit. Nearly all electronic arrangement respects 8-, 16-, or 32-bar boundaries — elements arrive and leave on phrase ends. "Phrase mixing" means aligning cue points to these boundaries so the structures of two tracks line up during a blend. ([Native Instruments on phrasing](https://blog.native-instruments.com/phrase-mixing/))

### The sections

- **Intro / mix-in** (16-32 bars). Stripped to kick + hats or a percussive loop. Designed for DJ overlay. "Mix in on the drums."
- **Groove / main body**. The full machine running — kick, bass, hats, main hook. "16 bars of rolling" means the track is sitting in this stable groove state; it's your mixing window.
- **First break / drop-out** (8-16 bars). Kick (often) vanishes; bass may remain. Tension via removal. Prime mix-out moment — "the bit where the kick drops out" lets the incoming track's kick carry solo.
- **Build-up** (8-16 bars). Noise sweeps, snare rolls, filter opens, risers. Tension climbs.
- **Drop** (16-32 bars). Full density re-engages. In EDM this is pyrotechnic; in house/techno it's simply the kick coming back with full groove.
- **Breakdown** (16-32 bars). Second, deeper dropout — usually atmospheric pads, no drums. In techno this is the hypnotic middle-break; in pop/disco it's the traditional **middle-8** bridge before the final chorus.
- **Peak / second drop**. Higher energy than first drop.
- **Outro / mix-out** (16-32 bars). Mirror of the intro — elements peel away until bare percussion remains. Designed for the next DJ/track.

([Attack Magazine arrangement](https://www.attackmagazine.com/), [Mixed In Key arrangement guide](https://mixedinkey.com/captain-plugins/wiki/how-to-arrange-a-dance-music-track/))

### Song-form terms (disco, hip-hop)

For **disco** and **hip-hop**, Western pop structure applies: *verse* (16 bars hip-hop / 8 bars disco) — *chorus/hook* (8 bars, repeated, higher-energy) — *bridge* or **middle-8** (8 bars, contrasting, before final chorus). The middle-8 is *not* the same as a techno breakdown — techno breakdowns drop drums entirely; a middle-8 swaps chord/melody while keeping groove.

### DJ technique vocabulary

- **Phrase mixing** — aligning to 16/32-bar boundaries so structures align
- **EQ trading** — swapping bass/mid/high EQ bands between two tracks during a long blend (bass first, to kill kick collision)
- **Harmonic mixing** — key-compatible tracks via Camelot wheel / Mixed In Key
- **Long blend** — 4+ minute transition across multiple phrases (house/progressive)
- **Cut mix** — abrupt swap on a downbeat (hip-hop, jungle, grime, footwork)

## Part 3 — Set-Level Structure: The Arc of the Night

Set structure is about shaping time. A 7-hour night has a physics — trust and energy accumulate, crowds peak and recover, and a DJ who violates the arc breaks the room. ([DJ TechTools timeline](https://djtechtools.com/2010/07/25/the-time-line-of-a-dance-floor/))

### Warm-up (10pm-12am, 110-122 BPM)

**Texture, not energy.** The warm-up DJ's job is atmospheric — create a *room* the headliner can walk into and take up. Standard etiquette: hold tempo and intensity *below* where the headliner will play. Never peak-hunt. No "biggest tracks." The RA feature ["The esoteric art of the opening DJ"](https://ra.co/features/1095) frames the warm-up as potentially the most important role of the night and the least respected.

A working rule: *familiar ≠ anthemic*. Accessible grooves, not bangers. Think about texture, tempo, groove, *and* energy independently — a track can be high-energy but textured-warm, or mid-tempo but tense. ([Decoded Magazine](https://www.decodedmagazine.com/art-warm-jock/), [DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2015/11/20/warmup-djs-play-to-the-crowd/))

### First peak (12am-1:30am, 122-128 BPM)

Room fills. First real energy spike. Recognizable but not the biggest moments. Classic rule: **never drop your biggest track before 1am** — trust hasn't been earned yet, and you've burned your ammunition.

### Climax / peak-time (1:30am-3:30am, 126-135 BPM house/techno)

Headliner's moment. Biggest tracks, largest tension-release swings. Cycles every 3-5 minutes. This is when you cash the trust the earlier hours earned.

### After-hours (3:30am-6am, or 10hr+ in Berlin)

Two schools. **Hypnotic deep**: long rolling grooves, 130-140 but minimal, deep, trance-inducing — Berghain main floor tradition, 10-hour closing sets. **Stay-peak intensity**: harder/faster material for the die-hards. Panorama Bar residents play 3+ hour sets to knowledgeable crowds. Less familiar material here — the remaining crowd is there for discovery, not hits. ([Berghain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghain))

### Closing (final 30-60 min, 110-120 BPM, slowing)

Ease out. Slow tempo to cool the floor. "End of night" emotional weight — classic vocals, disco staples, soul. Leave them feeling good. Full circle: closing tempos often mirror the warm-up, bookending the night.
