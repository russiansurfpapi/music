## The FADER — Rosalía's Real Magic (Cover Story, 2019)
URL: https://www.thefader.com/2019/05/29/rosalia-cover-story

**Genre/Sound:**
- "I understand that a lot of people can't connect with my music, because it's a radical proposal and a personal proposal."
- "If Latin music is music made in Spanish, then my music is part of Latin music."

**Flamenco Roots:**
- "From the beginning, I knew. I realized, _This is my path_."

**Vocal Philosophy:**
- "To me, everything comes from the voice. When I hear someone talk, when I hear someone communicate, the sound — it gives me a lot of information."
- "I love voices that are very old, very gravelly, very deep. I like metallic voices; I like velvety voices."

**Creative Process:**
- "I swear to you, it's like a need. It's like I always have a clear vision, and I always have to realize it."
- "I am experimental. It's a necessity for me. I don't know how to live any other way."

**Role in Music:**
- "There are artists of all kinds. There are artists who are here to remind us where we come from; there are artists who are here to show us where to go."

## Billboard — Meet Spain's Flamenco Revivalist (2018)
URL: https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/rosalia-flamenco-singer-interview-8471975/

**Flamenco Roots:**
- "Flamenco is the most honest and visceral music. You have to be sincere when you sing it. If you're not, it doesn't work."
- "The flamenco cantes (songs) are my foundation, my basis. Without flamenco, my music wouldn't make sense."

**Genre/Innovation:**
- "I try to do flamenco in tune with the moment that I'm living in, here and now."
- "It's a lot more interesting to dig into the popular music from where I'm from than adhere to some kind of standardized global pop."

**Identity:**
- "It's our roots that really give us our identity."

## Dazed — "Rosalía is the Catalan star reshaping the sound of global pop" (El Mal Querer, 2018)
URL: https://www.dazeddigital.com/music/article/42076/1/rosalia-el-mal-querer-interview

**Flamenco Roots:**
- "My music wouldn't make sense without flamenco. That's my base — flamenco is the classical foundation I chose to build my music on."
- "Flamenco inspiration is, above all, the frame I compose my music within."
- "I fell in love with this music genre when I was 13 and since then, I've never stopped exploring it."

**Genre/Innovation:**
- "I wanted to find a possible bridge between electronic sounds and heritage music."

**Album Concept (El Mal Querer):**
- "El Mal Querer focuses on passion, a dark love story is unveiled throughout the album, where each song is a chapter."

**Visual Aesthetic:**
- "Both videos are visual poems which — through the decontextualisation of the traditional elements in my culture — narrate two parts of the story."

**Generational Identity:**
- "My generation is especially connected to its roots and, simultaneously, to everything going on outside of Spain."

**Gender:**
- "The way I make music reflects the way I think. I'll never get tired of fighting until I see equal numbers of men and women in a recording session."

## Rolling Stone — "Rosalía on 'Motomami,' Reinventing Her Sound, and the Future of Pop" (2022)
URL: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-latin/rosalia-motomami-interview-1256304/

**Genre/Sound:**
- "Everything on this record was made from scratch. It's far from being expressly traditional."
- "I wanted to use different codes, I wanted to find another way of making a record this time."
- "Reggaeton has always been a part of my life since I was 11."
- "I don't like it when something sounds too preconceived."

**Vocal Approach:**
- "My voice is an instrument — it's absurd to use it in one way only."
- "How can I make this vocal sound more saturated than ever? How can I make this vocal sound really fragile?"
- "I've recorded a thousand vocals — some are the first take, but not most."

**Album Concept (Motomami):**
- "It's the most personal and confessional album that I've made so far."
- "In my head, MOTOMAMI makes sense as a concept, as a feminine figure building herself."

**Creative Process:**
- "I make music I like to listen to."
- "It would be too selfish of me to make music that only satisfies me."

**Influences:**
- "I didn't listen to flamenco until I was 13. When I listened to it, it was a turning point for me."

## Billboard — "Rosalía Talks New Album Motomami, Being In Love and Cooking" (2022)
URL: https://www.billboard.com/music/latin/rosalia-motomami-interview-1235047442/

**Album Concept (Motomami):**
- "Motomami is an energy. Anyone can be a Motomami, Motopapi. It's the way you feel."
- "It's doing as much as you can with what you have."
- "I wanted this roller coaster energy that is high and low, high and low."

**Creative Philosophy:**
- "I need to feel free to create and I need to create in order to feel free."
- "I make music because that's my truth."
- "Anything that is not honest, it's not in the album."
- "My biggest concern while I was doing the album was, I just want to be honest."

**Influences:**
- "When I was 15 or 14 even, I used to hear a lot of reggaetón. It's music that's always been there."
- "There's a huge, huge inspiration during the whole project. Latin America is a huge inspiration."
- "I love boleros. I can listen to boleros for hours."

## Billboard — Women In Music: Producer Of The Year (2022)
URL: https://www.billboard.com/music/features/rosalia-billboard-women-in-music-producer-of-the-year-interview-1235258160/

**Production Philosophy:**
- "My homework as a producer is to follow my intuition. It's to make decisions and take risks."
- "I try to not have a specific idea of how a song must sound. Instead, I go in with concepts, or _ilusiones_, of how I would like it to sound. But never a rigid idea. That's not organic, nor is it productive."
- "Producing also requires humility because you're constantly testing out ideas."

**On Learning Production:**
- "Oh, my God. I always felt that I wasn't good enough, so I would treat it more like a game."
- "I wanted to learn how to make a beat because when the time came that I needed to do it, I would be able to do it."

**On Freedom:**
- "One hundred percent yes. I was always thinking: 'How can I be freer?' Fear, or whatever the opposite of freedom is, is the biggest enemy for a creative."

**Women in Production:**
- "There's [still] skepticism about a woman being able to be an artist, singer, producer and songwriter at the same time. To me, these disciplines are not mutually exclusive."
- "If you don't see yourself represented in a role like this, how can you picture yourself working in that position?"

## Hola / Pitchfork — "Rosalía opens up about how she makes music" (2022)
URL: https://www.hola.com/us/entertainment/20220520332940/roslia-music-making-motomami-pitchfork/

**Creative Process:**
- "I'm not interested at all in any formula."
- "It's really hard for me not to create from this place of freedom."

**Work Regimen:**
- "It would be 16 hours a day, for months and months and months. It's been a tough process, I'm not going to lie to you."

**Influences / No Hierarchy:**
- "I love Miles Davis or el Coltrane the same way that I love Wisin & Yandel, Héctor, Lorna, or Ivy Queen."
- "I don't see music in a way that's like, Oh, this is right, this is wrong, this is better, this is worse."

**Emotional Standard:**
- "Does it give you goosebumps?"

**Mozart Parallel:**
- Referenced Mozart's creative freedom — inventing words, writing backwards, switching languages freely — as a model for her own process: "And that music would come from that freedom."

## PAPER Magazine — "Rosalía Talks El Mal Querer and Pedro Almodovar"
URL: https://www.papermag.com/rosalia-el-mal-querer

**Album Concept (El Mal Querer):**
- "El mal querer is a record with a story."
- "I've spent two years of my life on this."

**Flamenco:**
- "Life, love, fighting, spirituality, death — you can find everything in flamenco."
- "The flamenco inspiration is still there, but in a different way."
- "I'm so in love with the music. I would like everyone else to fall in love with that music."

**On "Malamente":**
- "Malamente is like when you have a feeling something bad is going to happen."
- "The tracks were coming from a very masculine place. Powerful."

**Creative Process:**
- "Sometimes it's the lyrics, sometimes it's melody, sometimes it's the production."
- "It's more like I'm about to go to sleep, and start getting relaxed, the melody appears."
- "I'm playing the bass lines... I'm choosing the chords."

**Influences:**
- "They are big references for all artists of my generation." — on Justin Timberlake and Beyoncé

**On Pedro Almodóvar:**
- "He's my very favorite artist in Spain. His energy is crazy."
- "It was very natural and organic to work together."

## Tom Tom Magazine — "Rosalía: Flamenco and her Fire" (2017)
URL: https://tomtommag.com/2017/06/rosalia-flamenco-fire/

**Flamenco Roots:**
- "For me flamenco is the form of expression that I have chosen."
- "I think it is different from other music because of its emotional weight, its depth."
- "Flamenco is 'root' music, it is folkloric."
- "I think that flamenco has to be modified whether we want it or not; just by the context."
- "It's not that I seek to change flamenco."

**Vocal Technique:**
- "You have to try to absorb how the rhythmic pattern is of each palo."
- "You have to be floating above the rhythm. You have to be almost surfing atop it."

**Artistic Honesty:**
- "I sing flamenco from my perspective."
- "I am just trying to explain flamenco in the most honest way possible."
- "I try to make music in the most transparent way I can."

**Mentor:**
- "From thirteen to sixteen I was listening to a lot of flamenco and at sixteen I found my mentor."
- "When I heard him sing I was amazed that he can sing any way he wants, he is very free."

## Remezcla — "With 'LUX,' Rosalía Turns Her Vision Away From Dembow & Towards a Heavenly Realm" (2025)
URL: https://remezcla.com/features/music/interview-with-lux-rosalia-turns-her-vision-away-from-dembow-towards-a-heavenly-realm/

**Spirituality/Music:**
- "I feel a deep commitment; it's like my mission in life is to make music."
- "The love I have for music is similar to the love I feel for God. They're very close."
- "God has blessed me so much in my life, and what better way to give back than by making a record?"

**Album Concept (Lux):**
- "I think it's something that's always been there, but this time, I felt truly prepared to create an entire project like this."
- "It's not even something totally new to me, because these themes have been present in my earlier work."

**Arrangements:**
- "[It was] the hardest [challenge] of my life! Honestly. I'd never done a project this big before."
- "The arrangements took almost a year to finish, many hours at the piano, refining everything."

**Multilingual Writing:**
- "Throughout the process of making this album, I approached the lyrics in the same way, no matter the language."
- "It was like solving a puzzle."

**Genre:**
- "Just the other day, my team was wondering what label do we put it under when it goes to stores, and, honestly, I don't know."
- "I'd rather let others decide where it belongs when they hear it."

## Billboard — Lux Cover Story (2025)
URL: https://www.billboard.com/music/pop/rosalia-lux-billboard-cover-story-interview-1236104956/

**Maximalism vs. Minimalism:**
- "Motomami was minimalist. This is maximalism."
- "I promised myself I was gonna make an album where I was going to finish the thought, finish the song, go all the way 'til the end."

**Orchestral Approach:**
- "This album has a completely different sound than any of the projects that I've done before. It was a challenge for me to do a more orchestral project."

**Lyrical Craft:**
- "It took me a year to write just the lyrics for this album, and then another year of arranging music and going back to the lyrics and retouching."
- "It has to sound good. There's a big difference for me when I write a letter than if I write a song."

**Writing Across 13 Languages:**
- "It took a lot of writing and scratching it and sending it to someone who would help me translate."

**Spirituality:**
- "The album is heavily inspired by the world of mysticism and spirituality. Since I was a kid, I've always had a very personal relationship with spirituality."

**Musical Philosophy:**
- "All of it is music. And if you understand that, then you can enjoy in a much fuller, profound way, what music is."
- "All the albums I've done helped me be able to be the musician I am today and make this album now."

**Creative Freedom:**
- "Success, for me, is freedom. And I felt all the freedom that I could imagine or hope for throughout this process."

**On Experimentation:**

- "His music has left a mark on me; not only the bold character of his production but also its minimalism and free structures."
