## 1. Nutrition Protocol

### The Problem You're Solving

Vyvanse suppresses appetite until ~6 PM, then rebounds hard. You undereat by day (500-800 cal by 6 PM), overeat at night (midnight Indian, 2 AM omelettes), bloat from FODMAP triggers, sleep poorly, wake heavy, repeat. The cycle costs you 2-3 lbs every bad night and takes a full week to recover.

### Your 5 Foundation Foods (adapted from Messi/Poser)

Poser built Messi's entire diet around five pillars. Yours are the same minus the FODMAP triggers:

1. **Water** — 3+ liters/day. Start every morning with warm lemon water (Messi and Slater both do this). Pre-hydrate before training, not during.
2. **Extra virgin olive oil** — on everything savory. Monounsaturated fat for sustained energy and anti-inflammatory properties.
3. **Whole grains** — brown rice, quinoa, oats. Slow-release energy that doesn't spike and crash. GF pasta for pre-soccer carb loads (Messi eats GF pasta 2-3h before matches).
4. **Fresh fruit** — mornings and early afternoon only. Bananas, berries, apples. Never after 8 PM (fructose ferments overnight = bloating).
5. **Fresh vegetables** — every lunch and dinner. Spinach, kale, zucchini, bell pepper, sweet potato, carrots. No broccoli (raffinose = FODMAP trigger).

**The Vyvanse fix is mechanical: set alarms for meals, eat by the clock, not by hunger.**

### Key Addition: Curry's Hip-Loading Principle

Your pronation issue is the same biomechanical problem Curry had with his ankles: **weak hips cause compensation downstream.** Curry's trainer Lyles discovered that building hip/glute strength (trap bar DL from 200→400 lbs) took stress off the ankles. Your weak glute medius drops your pelvis, which forces your foot to land crooked, which causes shin splints and stress fractures.

The fix is already in your circuit (one-leg DL, rear-foot elevated squat) but **add the hip airplane** — it's the single exercise Lyles credits most for Curry's transformation. It trains hip stability in rotation while standing on one leg, which is exactly what happens when you run and play soccer.

If you can access a trap bar at the YMCA: add trap bar deadlifts. Start light (135 lb), build slowly. This is Curry's primary compound lift and it directly strengthens the posterior chain that protects your feet.

## 3. Sleep Protocol

### The Facts

Every athlete in the research converges on the same answer: **sleep is the single highest-leverage intervention for performance, recovery, and weight loss.**

- Messi: 9 PM bedtime, 8-9 hours
- Curry: 11 PM-8 AM, no screens after 10:30 PM (sleep specialist Cheri Mah)
- Ronaldo: 90-minute sleep cycles, evolved to 7h main block + strategic naps
- Kane: Oura Ring data to decide push vs. rest days
- Slater: When practicing strict food combining, needs only 6 hours — but targets 8

You're getting 8+ hours only 2 out of 7 nights. This is the single biggest factor in stalling at 163. Short sleep (6-7h) means: wake up only 0.5-1 lb lighter instead of 2, elevated ghrelin (hunger hormone) all day, worse Vyvanse rebound, lower training quality, slower recovery.

### Protocol

**Standard nights (Sun, Wed, Thu, Fri, Sat):**
- 11:30 PM: Phone goes on charger in another room (Curry's no-screens rule, adapted to your schedule)
- 11:30 PM: Thought dump — open notes app, write whatever's in your head about the ex or anything else. Write until empty. Close the app. Don't reread it.
- 11:35 PM: Pick up a physical book. Read in bed.
- 12:00-12:15 AM: Lights off.
- Wake: 8:30-9:30 AM natural. Target 8-8.5 hours.

**CT pre-race nights (Monday, Tuesday):**
- These are your hardest nights. Treat them like Ronaldo treats match-eve: disciplined, scripted, no deviation.
- 10:30 PM: Phone on charger, no screens (matching Curry's 10:30 cutoff exactly)
- 10:30 PM: Thought dump
- 10:35 PM: Book
- 11:00 PM: Lights off, earplugs in (for Paul)
- Magnesium 300-400 mg at 10:00 PM (Messi takes this in the evening specifically for sleep quality)
- **Adderall booster must be taken before 2:00 PM on Monday and Tuesday** — if taken after 6 PM, it chemically prevents sleep before 12:30 AM, which means 7 hours max before the 8 AM alarm
- Target: asleep by 11:15 PM, wake 7:30 AM = 8.25 hours

**If Paul disrupts:** Earplugs are non-negotiable on Mon/Tue nights. If you don't have them, buy a 50-pack of foam earplugs this week. This is a $5 intervention that could fix 2 of your 7 nights.

**The Ronaldo nap option:** On days where you got 6-7 hours (after CT mornings), take a 20-30 minute nap between 2-4 PM if your schedule allows. Ronaldo's entire polyphasic system is built on using naps to supplement short nights. A 25-minute nap provides measurable cognitive and physical recovery without interfering with nighttime sleep.

### Sleep Tracking

Kane uses an Oura Ring to make data-driven decisions about push vs. rest. If you don't want to buy one ($300), use your phone's built-in sleep tracking (iOS Health app) or the free Sleep Cycle app. The point is: measure it. What gets measured gets managed. Right now "8+ hours only 2 out of 7 nights" is a guess — verify it, then watch the number climb as you implement the protocol.

## 4. Recovery Protocol

### Active Injury Management

**Metatarsal pain (left foot, top, started after a run):**
- Stop running on it until it's pain-free for 3 consecutive days. Slater surfed 30 years on a torn labrum and needed a hip replacement at 53 — don't be Slater with injuries. Be Curry: address it early and structurally.
- Swim instead. Pull-buoy sets with ankle weights (Ronaldo's arms-only protocol) give you a full cardio session with zero foot impact.
- Ice 15 min after any activity, 2-3x daily.
- If it persists past 2 weeks, see a sports medicine doctor. Metatarsal stress reactions become stress fractures if you run through them — you already know this from your history.

**Adductor strain:**
- Continue the stretching protocol (seated straddle, butterfly, kneeling hip flexor, 90/90).
- Inner thigh plank (Copenhagen) at reduced intensity — 15-20s instead of 25-30s until strain resolves.
- Messi's approach: resistance band hip flexor training, not heavy loaded adductor work. Strengthen around the injury, don't test it.

### Gum Graft Recovery Weeks (Early June, Early July)

**Days 1-3:** Full rest. Soft foods only — smoothies (almond milk, banana, berries, plant protein), oatmeal, mashed sweet potato, scrambled eggs, soups (no garlic/onion — use ginger, chives, turmeric). Keep protein intake up via shakes.

**Days 4-5:** Light pool swimming. Pull-buoy only, no flip turns (blood pressure). 1,500-2,000 yards easy. This is your Ronaldo/Slater recovery day — water as medicine, not training.

**Days 6-7:** Resume gym circuit at 75% intensity (3 rounds instead of 4, lighter weight). Resume easy running if mouth allows. No soccer until fully cleared.

**Weight during recovery weeks:** Hold steady. Don't try to cut during surgical recovery — your body needs calories to heal. Any loss is a bonus. Strength gains hold through a 1-week break without significant loss (research confirms this).

## 5. The 8 Rules

These are your daily non-negotiables. Every one traces directly to verified athlete practice.

### 1. Eat lunch by 1 PM, even if you're not hungry.
Set the alarm. 400-600 calories minimum. This is the single most important intervention for breaking the Vyvanse-rebound-midnight-eating cycle. Ronaldo eats every 3-4 hours by the clock. Messi's largest meal is lunch. Neither of them waits until they're hungry.

### 2. Kitchen closes at 9 PM.
After 9: water, almonds, herbal tea. Nothing else. No fruit (fructose ferments). No dairy (lactose ferments). No Indian food. No exceptions except post-soccer recovery meal (which should be eaten by 10 PM). Ronaldo's chef Barone: "Never go to bed with a full stomach."

### 3. Phone on charger at 11:30 PM (10:30 on CT nights).
Curry's rule via sleep specialist Cheri Mah. Not "I'll just check one thing." On the charger, in another room if possible. Book in hand, lights off 30 min later.

### 4. Pronation drills every day, no exceptions.
10 minutes, barefoot. This is your equivalent of Ronaldo's 200-300 daily crunches or Messi's 1+ hour of stretching. The small daily investment that prevents the catastrophic injury (stress fracture) that derails everything for 6 months.

### 5. Front-load calories, back-load water.
Breakfast and lunch should contain 60-70% of your daily calories. Dinner should be the lightest meal. After 8 PM, drink water instead of eating. Every athlete in the research eats their heaviest meal early and their lightest at night.

### 6. No medium effort.
80% easy, 20% hard. Easy runs at 135-145 BPM — truly easy. Speed work at full effort. Nothing in between. "Medium" effort is too hard to recover from and too easy to improve from. Ronaldo's circuits are moderate weight but high intensity with minimal rest. Messi walks the most and runs the least during matches — saving energy for decisive sprints.

### 7. Sleep 8+ hours, 5 out of 7 nights.
Measure it. Track it in your phone or with a ring. If you hit 8+ hours on 5 nights this week, you'll weigh less on Friday than you did on Monday. If you hit it on only 2 nights, you'll oscillate. This is the one variable that everything else depends on.

### 8. One bad day can't become two.
Every athlete has bad days. Kane eats pizza after matches. Messi admits he craves sweets. The difference is: one bad meal doesn't become a bad week. If you eat midnight Indian food on a Wednesday, Thursday morning starts with the lemon water and oatmeal, not "fuck it, I already blew it." The oscillation pattern (5-6 good days, 1-2 bad days that erase the deficit) breaks when the bad days get less bad — not when the good days get more restrictive.
